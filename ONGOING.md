# Living with it — the ongoing practice

> Setup is a day. The value is the habit. Here's the loop, what to connect, and the part most people get wrong: **what context is actually worth keeping.**

---

## What's the *right* context to keep

Not everything. A brain stuffed with noise is as useless as an empty one. Keep what is **(a) true, (b) yours to know, and (c) worth being current.** In practice, five kinds:

- **Goals** — what you're trying to achieve, and where you actually stand. Everything points here.
- **The relationships that matter** — customers, partners, advisors: who they are, what they want, the last real exchange. Not every contact — the ones that move a goal.
- **Decisions + why** — what you chose and the reasoning, so you and the AI don't relitigate it next month.
- **What you've learned** — the lesson from a win or a loss, written once, so the next conversation starts ahead.
- **The current state** — where each thing genuinely stands today.

**Leave out:** every passing message, anything you can't keep current, anything that's just noise. The test: *if you wouldn't act on it, it doesn't belong.*

## What systems to connect (and when)

Connect a system when it **owns a fact your OS needs** — not just because you can.

- **Start with one:** your calendar (so the brief knows your day), or your meeting notes (so conversations get captured automatically).
- **Then your source of truth:** your CRM / system-of-record if you're B2B; your money dashboard — Stripe, Shopify, analytics — if you're transactional.
- **Then comms:** email/inbox, for drafting and follow-up.

If a connection doesn't make a routine smarter, skip it. More plumbing you don't watch is just more places to break.

## The ongoing rhythm (it's mostly the routines)

- **After anything real happens** — a call, a decision, a number lands — tell the OS, or drop the transcript in. Capture *everything*; but the gate (`INGESTION_GATE.md`) decides what counts as fact, and asks you **how much weight** each thing deserves — a vendor's flattery and a customer saying they're leaving are not equal signals.
- **Weekly, it asks you** what changed that it couldn't see (the reconciliation loop). Five minutes. That's you keeping it honest.
- **The routines do the rest** — re-scoring goals, surfacing what's slipping, drafting the next move, auditing for rot.
- **You stay the judge** — you send, you approve, you decide.

The discipline is small: **feed it what matters, answer its weekly questions, let it work.** Skip that and it quietly goes stale — a fresh-looking brain that's wrong. Keep it, and every week it's a little sharper than the last.
